Selling in the fall comes with its own timing questions, and a few early decisions can be hard to undo once a listing has sat. Here are seven mistakes sellers can make, and what to do instead.

First, a look at the local numbers, because they shape everything below. In Franklin, the median sale price was $863,000, up 12.8% from a year earlier, with a median of 49 days on market and an average sale-to-list ratio of 98.3% (Redfin, rolling 3 months, data as of 2026-08-31). Those are citywide figures. Your home, price range and street can look different.

## Pricing mistakes: old comps and ignored competition

![Photo: Modern kitchen, interior, minimalist design, kitchen island, open concept](/img/blog/selling-home-franklin-tn-fall-mistakes/inline-1.jpg)

*Photo by [WindraPy](https://pixabay.com/users/WindraPy-22300521/) on [Pixabay](https://pixabay.com)*

**Mistake one: pricing from old comps.** A sale from six months ago tells you what a buyer paid then. It does not tell you what a buyer will pay this month. Zillow Research shows Franklin's Market Temperature Index at 46, down 6 points over three months, which points to a cooling trend (Zillow Research, as of August 31, 2026). Pricing from a spring sale can leave you above where buyers are now.

Start with the most recent closed sales of similar homes, and give the newest ones the most weight. Your agent can pull these from [RealTracs MLS](https://www.realtracs.com). You can also check public records yourself on the [Williamson County property search](https://www.williamsoncounty-tn.gov/2099/35739/Property-Data-Search).

The pricing question is rarely about what you need to net. It is about what the market is paying for a home like yours right now. If you want a starting point, the [home value page](/sell#form) is a good place to begin.

**Mistake two: ignoring active listings and price cuts.** Closed sales show the past. Active listings show your real competition. Redfin reports 531 active listings in Franklin and 3.4 months of supply (Redfin, rolling 3 months, as of 2026-08-31). Months of supply is how long it would take to sell every active listing at the current pace. Under 4 months favors sellers, 4 to 6 is balanced, and over 6 favors buyers.

A citywide number does not mean your price range moves at the same pace. Look at what is listed near you, how long those homes have sat, and which ones have dropped their price. A listing that has been cut twice is telling you something about where buyers drew the line. The [Franklin housing market data](/franklin/housing-market) page is one place to track the broader picture.

## Presentation mistakes: curb appeal, renovations and photos

**Mistake three: skipping curb appeal.** Curb appeal is the first impression a buyer gets in person. Fall brings leaves, dull beds and fading paint on trim. Raking, pressure washing, fresh mulch, a clean front door and working exterior lights are modest expenses. Simple, tidy and well lit is the goal.

**Mistake four: over-renovating before you list.** A big kitchen or bath project right before listing can eat your net proceeds, and there is no guarantee you will get that money back in the sale price. Repairs buyers will notice anyway, such as a stained ceiling, a broken fixture or peeling paint, are usually the better use of money. Before you spend on anything large, ask your agent how similar homes in your price range have sold with and without that upgrade.

**Mistake five: weak photos.** Photos are a big part of how a listing is presented online. Dark rooms, cluttered counters, a car in the driveway and photos taken at dusk make a home look smaller and less cared for. Clear the surfaces, turn on every light, and shoot in daylight. Fall days get shorter, so schedule photos earlier in the day. Also make sure the listing has complete details, including accurate square footage and room counts.

## Offer mistakes: looking only at the price

![Photo: House, architecture, front yard, garage, home](/img/blog/selling-home-franklin-tn-fall-mistakes/inline-2.jpg)

*Photo by [Pexels](https://pixabay.com/users/Pexels-2286921/) on [Pixabay](https://pixabay.com)*

**Mistake six: rejecting an offer without weighing the full terms.** The price is only one line. An offer also includes:

- the financing type, such as conventional, FHA or VA
- the earnest money amount
- the closing date
- inspection and repair requests
- any request for the seller to pay closing costs or fund a rate buydown

Buyers can ask for help with closing costs or a buydown, and whether that works depends on the seller, the home, the market and what the buyer's lender allows. Ask your agent and the buyer's lender what is possible before you respond.

A slightly lower price with a clean, quick close can leave you in a different spot than a higher price with a long list of contingencies. Compare offers by what you would actually walk away with and how likely each is to reach closing. Your agent can help you lay them side by side. For tax questions, talk with a tax professional.

Also expect the usual requests. Termite and wood-destroying organism inspections are commonly requested by buyers and lenders in Tennessee, and the residential property condition disclosure (or exemption) is part of the sale. If your home was built before 1978, the lead-based paint disclosure applies.

## Planning mistake: no plan if early interest is slow

**Mistake seven: having no plan for a slow start.** It is easy to plan for offers and forget to plan for silence. If showings are light, do not just wait.

Decide before you list when you will review. At that point, sit down with your agent and look at:

- showing counts and any feedback
- how your photos and description compare to nearby listings
- new competing listings and recent price changes
- whether the price still fits the latest closed sales

Nashville metro numbers give a wider view. Redfin shows 5.4 months of supply and a median of 68 days on market for the four weeks ending 2026-09-27, with a sale-to-list ratio of 97.8% (Redfin, Nashville metro). That is a more balanced picture than Franklin's 3.4 months, which is a reminder that conditions differ by area and price range. See the [Redfin Data Center](https://www.redfin.com/news/data-center/) for current figures. Realtor.com Research puts Williamson County hotness at 41.7 of 100, a county-wide figure (Realtor.com Research, September 2026).

None of this predicts what your listing will do. It is a reason to have a plan rather than a hope.
