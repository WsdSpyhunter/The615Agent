When people ask about the cost of living in Williamson County, they usually start with home prices and the mortgage payment. That is a fair place to start, but it is only one line in the monthly budget.

Here is what else belongs on the list, so the number you plan around is the number you actually pay.

## Start with the full monthly picture

![Photo: House, garage, driveway, architecture, building](/img/blog/cost-of-living-williamson-county-beyond-the-mortgage/inline-1.jpg)

*Photo by [Pexels](https://pixabay.com/users/Pexels-2286921/) on [Pixabay](https://pixabay.com)*

A mortgage payment is principal and interest. Your real housing payment also includes property taxes, homeowner's insurance, and private mortgage insurance if it applies. Private mortgage insurance is typically required on conventional loans when the down payment is under 20 percent.

The rate matters too. The 30-year fixed mortgage rate was 7.40% for the week of October 8, 2026 (Freddie Mac via FRED, Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis). Nobody can say where rates go from here, so budget with today's number rather than a hoped-for one.

Price levels differ by city. In Franklin, the median sale price was $863,089, up 12.8% from a year earlier (Redfin, rolling 3 months, data as of September 30, 2026). In Spring Hill, it was $499,752, down 5.7% from a year earlier (Redfin, rolling 3 months, data as of September 30, 2026). Those are citywide medians, and any single home can sit far from them.

To test a payment with your own numbers, try the [Mortgage calculator](/mortgage-calculator). For a broader look at one city, see the [Franklin cost of living](/franklin/cost-of-living) page.

## Property taxes and insurance depend on the address

I can't give you a single tax figure for the county, and I won't guess at one. Property taxes depend on the exact address, so verify them with the county for any home you are serious about.

Spring Hill is a good example. It includes parts of both Williamson and Maury counties, so county services, schools and taxes can differ from one address to the next. The [City of Spring Hill](https://www.springhilltn.org) and City of Franklin sites are good starting points for city-level information.

School assignment works the same way. It depends on the address, so confirm it with the school district. For homes in the Williamson County district, start with [Williamson County Schools](https://www.wcs.edu).

Homeowner's insurance is priced for the specific home, so ask for quotes once you have an address in mind. If you are putting less than 20 percent down on a conventional loan, ask your lender what the private mortgage insurance would add each month. A lender can also explain how FHA or VA loans handle mortgage insurance, since the rules are different.

## Utilities and upkeep

![Photo: Interior, living room, furniture, neoclassical, design](/img/blog/cost-of-living-williamson-county-beyond-the-mortgage/inline-2.jpg)

*Photo by [4787421](https://pixabay.com/users/4787421-4787421/) on [Pixabay](https://pixabay.com)*

Utility bills vary with the size, age and systems of the home. Typical monthly lines include electric, water and sewer, trash, and internet. Which provider serves a home depends on the address. Depending on where you buy, electric service may come from a provider such as [Middle Tennessee Electric](https://www.mte.com), so confirm it for the home you are considering.

A practical step is to ask whether past utility bills are available for a home you like. Even a rough idea beats guessing.

Then there is maintenance. Homes need repairs and replacements over time, and a renter never sees those bills. A thorough inspection helps you see what you are taking on. Common add-ons to a standard home inspection are radon testing, a sewer scope and a termite inspection. Termite and wood-destroying organism inspections are commonly requested by buyers and lenders in Tennessee.

If you are comparing buying with renting, compare the full monthly cost of buying (payment, taxes, insurance, maintenance) against renting a comparable home. Then weigh how long you expect to stay.

## Commute costs

Commute costs are easy to leave out. They include fuel, wear on your vehicle, and the time itself. Two homes at the same price can have very different commutes.

Before you commit to an area, drive the route you would actually take, at the hours you would actually drive it. You can also check current conditions on [TDOT SmartWay traffic](https://smartway.tn.gov) to see how the roads look at different times.

If you are weighing a few communities along the I-65 corridor, the [Market data for the I-65 corridor](/market-data) page is one place to compare local numbers. The [Franklin area guide](/areas/franklin) and [Spring Hill area guide](/areas/spring-hill) are also on the site.

## One-time costs and a cushion

Some costs show up once, but they are large enough to plan for.

- **Closing costs** typically run 2 to 4 percent of the purchase price, plus earnest money and moving costs.
- **Moving:** request quotes from at least 3 licensed moving companies, and book early for month-end dates.
- **Loan paperwork:** a lender must send the Closing Disclosure at least 3 business days before closing. Compare it with your Loan Estimate and ask questions.

Two other habits protect your budget. From the time you apply for a loan through closing, avoid opening new credit, financing furniture, or changing jobs. And plan for an emergency fund after closing, with stable income and a payment that fits comfortably. Many buyers find that readiness and monthly budget are better guides than trying to time the market.
