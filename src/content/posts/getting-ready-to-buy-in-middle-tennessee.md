Buying a home can feel like a hundred decisions at once. The good news is that the first few steps are simple, and doing them in the right order saves time and stress.

## Start with your real budget

Get pre-approved before you start touring. Pre-approval means a lender has reviewed your income, credit, and documents, so you know what you can actually borrow. It is not the same as pre-qualification. Sellers take pre-approved buyers more seriously, and you will not fall in love with a home you cannot afford.

Then build your cash-to-close fund. Closing costs typically run 2 to 4 percent of the purchase price, plus earnest money and moving costs. You can [estimate your monthly payment with the mortgage calculator](/mortgage-calculator).

## Decide what matters

Write down your must-haves, your nice-to-haves, and your dealbreakers. Then research the areas you are considering: neighborhoods, school districts, commute times, HOA rules and fees, and typical property tax and utility costs. School assignment and taxes depend on the exact address, so verify them for each home you are serious about.

## Protect your finances

From the day you apply through closing, avoid opening new credit, financing furniture, or changing jobs. Lenders look at your finances again before closing, and surprises can delay or derail a loan.

## Find a Realtor you trust

Sign a buyer representation agreement with someone who will explain the process and answer every question, big or small. Ask how they communicate, how they handle negotiations, and what happens at each stage.
