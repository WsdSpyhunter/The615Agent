When the Federal Reserve makes news, buyers tend to ask the same thing: does this change my mortgage rate? The Fed is one influence among several. It does not set the rate on your loan.

This post explains what the Fed does and does not control, what else shapes the rate you are quoted, and how to plan around a number nobody can reliably predict. I won't guess where rates are headed, and you should be wary of anyone who does.

## What the Fed does and does not control

![Photo: Real estate, homeownership, homebuying, home, house](/img/blog/federal-reserve-mortgage-rates-explained/inline-1.jpg)

*Photo by [OleksandrPidvalnyi](https://pixabay.com/users/OleksandrPidvalnyi-4638469/) on [Pixabay](https://pixabay.com)*

The Fed sets short-term policy rates. A mortgage is a long-term loan, and the rate on yours is set by the lender you choose, based on market conditions and the details of your loan.

Federal Reserve Governor Michael S. Barr said in a September 23 speech:

> “Our short-term policy rates affect longer-term borrowing rates, including those for mortgages, but many other things affect mortgage rates as well.”
>
> — Michael S. Barr, Federal Reserve Governor, in a speech on September 23, 2026 ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/barr20260923a.htm))

That is a useful way to think about a Fed announcement. Policy rates are one input, and other factors also play a part. A headline about a Fed rate cut does not tell you what a lender will quote you on a given day.

## What else shapes the rate you are quoted

Because many things affect mortgage rates, two buyers in the same town can see different numbers on the same day. Here are the pieces I can point to from reliable sources:

- **The lender.** The CFPB encourages borrowers to get Loan Estimates from more than one lender and compare them, because interest rates and fees can differ from lender to lender.
- **The loan type.** Conventional, FHA, VA and adjustable-rate loans work differently. An adjustable-rate mortgage (ARM) has a rate that is fixed for an initial period and can then change. It can lower the early payment but carries the risk of higher payments later. You can read about [VA home loans](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans/) on the official page.
- **Your down payment and finances.** Private mortgage insurance is typically required on a conventional loan when the down payment is under 20 percent. FHA loans allow as little as 3.5 percent down.
- **Available programs.** The [Tennessee Housing Development Agency](https://www.thda.org) offers home loan programs through participating lenders, so ask any lender you are considering whether they take part.

Lenders include banks, credit unions, mortgage brokers and online lenders. A mortgage broker compares offers from several lenders on your behalf. You choose your own lender, and you are free to use any lender you like.

## How to compare offers

![Photo: House, garage, driveway, architecture, building](/img/blog/federal-reserve-mortgage-rates-explained/inline-2.jpg)

*Photo by [Pexels](https://pixabay.com/users/Pexels-2286921/) on [Pixabay](https://pixabay.com)*

After you apply, a lender must give you a Loan Estimate within three business days. It is a standard three-page form, so estimates from different lenders can be compared side by side. The [CFPB Owning a Home guides](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/) walk through how to read it.

Credit-scoring models generally treat several mortgage inquiries made within a short period as a single inquiry. Comparing lenders close together is usually less harmful to your credit than spreading applications out. Ask each lender how this works for your situation.

Remember that a pre-approval letter is not a final loan commitment. Final approval depends on the appraisal, underwriting and your finances staying the same until closing. That means no new credit, no financing furniture and no job changes while your loan is in process.

## Where rates and local numbers stand

For reference, Freddie Mac reported the 30-year fixed mortgage rate at 7.40% for the week of 2026-10-08, according to Freddie Mac data on FRED. That is a national weekly figure, not a quote for you.

Local conditions differ too. Redfin reports that in the Nashville metro area, for the 4 weeks ending 2026-10-11, the median sale price was $466,593, median days on market was 66, the sale-to-list ratio was 97.7% and months of supply was 5.2.

Months of supply is how long it would take to sell every active listing at the current pace of sales. Under 4 months favors sellers, 4 to 6 months is balanced, and over 6 months favors buyers.

Closer to Williamson County, Redfin data as of 2026-09-30 (rolling 3 months) shows Franklin with a median sale price of $863,089, up 12.8% from a year earlier, and 4.0 months of supply. Spring Hill shows a median sale price of $499,752, down 5.7% from a year earlier, and 4.1 months of supply.

Spring Hill includes parts of both Williamson and Maury counties, so county services, schools and taxes can differ by address. Verify them with the school district and the county for any home you are serious about.

Those numbers differ a lot from city to city, which is why national rate headlines only tell part of the story. The [market data for the I-65 corridor](/market-data) page can help you look at your own area.

## If you are waiting for a rate cut

Many buyers wait for rates to fall. Nobody can reliably predict where rates will go, so waiting is a bet in either direction. The cost of waiting is real even though it is easy to overlook: rent paid, price changes, or losing a home you wanted.

Some buyers buy now and plan to refinance later. Refinancing is not guaranteed. It depends on rates, credit, home value and income at that time, and it has closing costs of its own. Treat it as a possibility, not a plan.

There are other questions worth asking in the meantime:

- Can the seller pay some closing costs or fund a temporary or permanent rate buydown? That depends on the seller, the home and the market, and a lender must confirm what is allowed for your loan.
- Is the home's FHA or VA loan assumable? This is uncommon to find and has its own requirements, so it is worth asking about but should not be counted on.

A different way to frame the decision is personal readiness: stable income, an emergency fund after closing, and a payment that fits comfortably. You can test payments at different rates with the [mortgage calculator](/mortgage-calculator). Some buyers also compare the full monthly cost of owning (payment, taxes, insurance, maintenance) with renting a comparable home, and weigh how long they expect to stay.

For general buyer education, [HUD's buying a home page](https://www.hud.gov/topics/buying_a_home) is another official place to start.
